Day one to one year

The big picture, without the medical jargon.

These are common changes families notice. A baby can be healthy and still take a different route through them.

Day 1

Hello, world.

Most newborns are curled up, sleepy and driven by reflexes. They already know familiar voices and smells, and feeding and skin-to-skin are the main events.

☼
Days 2–3

Settling in.

A little early weight loss is common. Baby is learning to feed, stay warm and adjust to life outside the womb.

◌
Days 4–7

Finding a rhythm.

Feeds and wake-ups still feel unpredictable. Wet nappies and feeding patterns become useful clues that baby is taking in enough.

≈
Week 2

Back toward birth weight.

Many babies are moving back toward their birth weight. They may stare at your face for a few seconds and calm to your voice.

♡
Weeks 3–4

More awake moments.

Baby may briefly lift the head during tummy time, look at faces and have slightly longer quiet-alert periods.

◎
2 months • CDC checkpoint

Smiles begin to mean something.

Many babies look at faces, react to sound, smile when you engage them and hold the head up for short periods during tummy time.

☺
3 months

More conversation.

Cooing, hand-to-mouth play and longer eye contact become more obvious. Head control is getting steadier.

♪
4 months • CDC checkpoint

“Look at me!”

Baby may make sounds or movements to keep your attention, hold the head steady, bring hands to mouth and push up on the forearms.

✦
5 months

Grabbing everything.

Hands get busier. Baby may reach for toys, grab feet, laugh more and roll in one or both directions.

✋
6 months • CDC checkpoint

A more social little person.

Many babies know familiar people, laugh, reach for wanted toys and roll. Around this age, solids may begin when baby shows readiness while milk remains important.

●
7–8 months

Sitting changes the view.

Many babies sit more steadily, transfer objects between hands and explore everything with hands and mouth. Some begin scooting or rocking.

△
9 months • CDC checkpoint

Baby knows who is who.

Many babies look when their name is called, sit without support and show clear preferences for familiar people. Stranger wariness can be normal.

◇
10 months

Busy hands, busy brain.

Picking up small things gets more precise. Baby may copy sounds, gesture, crawl or find another way to move around.

⋆
11 months

Practising independence.

Baby may pull up, cruise along furniture, wave, clap and understand familiar routines. Not every baby crawls before walking.

↗
12 months • CDC checkpoint

One whole year.

Many one-year-olds play simple games, use “mama” or “dada” as a name, put objects into containers and pull to stand. Some take steps; many do not yet.

A simple thing to do

This is a guide, not a diagnosis. If your baby loses a skill they had before, or something about their development worries you, talk with a child-health professional.
Physical growth

Growth is a curve, not a single “perfect” number.

One measurement tells you very little by itself. The useful information is whether your baby's measurements are following a steady pattern over time.

What gets measured?

WHO growth standards include separate charts for boys and girls. Health workers usually follow several measurements together rather than judging a baby by weight alone.

⚖
WeightNewborns commonly lose some weight in the first days. Many regain birth weight by about 2 weeks; almost all by about 3 weeks.
↕
LengthMeasured lying down in infancy. The trend is compared with age and sex on a growth chart.
◯
Head circumferenceThis helps clinicians follow skull and brain growth, especially during the rapid early months.
↗
The pattern over timeA healthy child may be naturally small or large. What matters is the overall growth pattern and the child's health, feeding and development.

Keep the chart, lose the comparison.

Your baby's growth should be compared with their own pattern, not with another baby's size.

Care notes

What matters more than hitting a milestone “on time.”

How baby looks, feeds, breathes, responds and changes over time matters. Trust a concern that something is not right.

Everyday safety

For sleep, place baby on the back on a firm, flat sleep surface with a fitted sheet and keep soft bedding and toys out of the sleep space.

  • Use tummy time only while baby is awake and supervised.
  • Introduce complementary foods around 6 months when developmentally ready.
  • Keep routine well-child visits so growth and development can be followed over time.

Get medical help promptly if…

Seek urgent care for trouble breathing, blue/grey colour, extreme sleepiness or difficulty waking, repeated vomiting with poor fluid intake, or if your baby seems seriously unwell.

  • A baby younger than 3 months with a temperature of 38°C (100.4°F) or higher needs prompt medical evaluation.
  • Talk to a clinician if your baby loses a skill they previously had or you are worried about development.
